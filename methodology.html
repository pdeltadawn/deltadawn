Methodology

How This Archive
Was Made

This project documents objects from the Textiles + Fiber collection at the University of Kansas through photogrammetry—a process of photographing objects from multiple angles and computationally stitching those images into 3D models.

The resulting models allow viewers to rotate, zoom, and examine surface details that flat photography cannot capture: the topography of stitching, the interaction of weave structures with light, the dimensionality of coiled or plaited forms. At the same time, significant information is lost. Weight, flexibility, smell, sound, and texture under the hand are absent from any digital surrogate.

Objects were selected based on accessibility, condition, and the range of techniques represented. Many lack complete provenance—a common condition in institutional textile collections, where makers' names, acquisition contexts, and cultural histories were often not recorded. This project does not invent information to fill those gaps. Where knowledge is incomplete, that incompleteness is documented.

I · Process

Building the Archive

3D models were processed using Agisoft Metashape. The website was built with assistance from AI tools. Research on individual objects is ongoing.

This archive was developed through an iterative process of scanning, writing, and revision. Some choices—what to include, how to frame objects whose histories are incomplete, when to leave questions open rather than invent answers—emerged through that process rather than being determined in advance.

II · Photogrammetry

How the Objects
Become Models

Each object in this archive exists first as a physical thing—handled, worn, traded, carried. The 3D scan is an attempt to hold something of that presence in a form that can be shared. This section describes the process: what the scanner captures, what it misses, and what that gap reveals.

What the Scanner Captures

Photogrammetry is partial by nature. The scan captures geometry and surface—but not weight, not smell, not the softness of fiber under a hand. Some materials cooperate; others resist. This is not a failure of the technology so much as a documentation of its particular kind of looking.

Captures

  • Surface geometry and dimension
  • Weave structure and pattern
  • Three-dimensional form
  • Color and tonal variation

Resists capture

  • Weight and drape
  • Texture under the hand
  • Smell
  • Sound of the cloth
  • Flexibility and movement

III · The Collection

Where These Objects
Come From

The objects in this exhibition come from the physical collection of the Textiles + Fiber area at the University of Kansas. They were acquired over decades, by hands other than mine, for reasons not always recorded.

The KU Textiles + Fiber Collection

The collection includes woven, stitched, and constructed textiles from a range of origins and time periods. It is used for teaching and research. Many items lack complete provenance—a common condition in institutional textile collections, where makers' names, acquisition contexts, and cultural histories were often not recorded at the time of acquisition.

The full collection is catalogued in the KU Textiles Omeka database.

Selection

Objects were selected based on accessibility, condition, and the range of techniques represented. The archive does not present a neutral cross-section of the collection. Selection is always curatorial.

On Incompleteness

Where knowledge is incomplete, that incompleteness is documented rather than smoothed over. The archive does not invent provenance or attribution. Gaps in the record are part of the record.
